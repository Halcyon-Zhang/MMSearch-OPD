01 / Motivation

Why existing questions are not enough.

The training gap. Long-horizon multimodal search needs harder questions and process feedback. Existing datasets are often solvable from parametric knowledge or a short retrieval, while a terminal reward says little about redundant verification, premature stopping, or an unsupported answer.

02 / Overview

Easy questions made hard, then made learnable.

MMSearch-OPD refines an easy image–question pair, then trains with joint RL and on-policy distillation (OPD). BuildLink extracts the entity chain, Expand extends it, Blur adds disambiguation, Visualize introduces retrievable visual evidence, and Synthesis rewrites the question with indirect clues. Answer rewards and behavior penalties guide RL, while the reward gap weights token-level OPD on imperfect student trajectories.

03 / Performance

Stronger search at 9B, and a stronger 27B teacher.

On seven multimodal search benchmarks, MMSearch-OPD-9B (RL) reaches 60.2 average accuracy, 15.3 points above the Qwen3.5-9B agentic workflow and 9.6 points above DeepVoyager-VL-8B. The 27B SFT teacher reaches 63.2, 6.5 points above its own agentic workflow.

04 / Refine

The same training, on questions Refine has made harder.

Refine is read in two ways. One comparison trains the same model on questions before and after the full pipeline. The other follows those questions through each stage.

Before and after refine

Both runs start from Qwen3.5-9B and share the SFT configuration and training budget. Only the questions change. The model trained on refined questions is stronger on all four benchmarks. The largest gain is +15.0 on BrowseComp-V³, and average tool calls per trajectory rise from 6.61 to 33.71.

Stage by stage

On the same 100 questions, Seed2.1-Pro accuracy falls from 79% before Refine to 65% after Expand, 44% after Blur, and 33% after Visualize. Turns rise from 9.8 to 20.2, and tool calls from 18.4 to 34.7.

05 / Training

Joint training adds accuracy, then spends less search.

Joint RL–OPD lifts the 9B SFT average from 57.9 to 60.2, with a gain on every one of the seven benchmarks. Later in training, rollouts use fewer tool calls and fewer tokens while answer reward stays comparable.

On-policy distillation

A frozen 27B teacher guides the student on its own rollouts. For each question the student samples a group. Correctness c and a penalty H for wasteful search form the reward, and reinforcement learning uses the group-centered gap.

Distillation runs only when a rollout is not fully correct. The reward gap sets the token-level strength, and fully correct trajectories get none. The weight λ then decays, so later updates rely more on the outcome.
